> An RFC explores a **direction** for a big or ambiguous piece of work *before* committing to an
> implementation design. It is lighter than a Proposal and exists to think out loud, surface
> alternatives, and align on the approach. An accepted RFC graduates into a Proposal (Article 0) for
> implementation and/or an ADR for the decision.

## Problem
What's wrong or missing, and why it matters now. Who feels it.

## Goal
What "good" looks like. The outcome, not the implementation.

## Non-goals
Explicitly out of scope — what this RFC is *not* trying to solve.

## Alternatives
Two or more directions, each with trade-offs. Include "do nothing."

## Risks
What could go wrong; unknowns; what we'd need to validate.

## Migration / rollout sketch
High-level only — enough to know it's feasible. (The Proposal does the detail.)

## Decision
The chosen direction and why. Then: does this need a Proposal (Article 0), an ADR, or both?
